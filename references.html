Evidence and mechanics registry

This table separates established scientific principles from illustrative game rules. Numerical parameters and probabilities in this game are illustrative and should not be quoted as laboratory measurements or recommended experimental conditions.

Polymer specificity and sorting

Evidence: PETase belongs to a group of enzymes with substrate selectivity; PET and polyolefins such as PE/PP differ chemically. Game mechanism: PET proceeds to a contained reactor; PE/PP must be diverted. Sources: Yoshida et al. (2016), Austin et al. (2018).

PET crystallinity, surface accessibility and pretreatment

Evidence: Material properties and access can significantly affect enzymatic PET hydrolysis. Game mechanism: Size, crystallinity, additives and accessibility modify a conceptual processing rate. Important caveat: Numerical multipliers are invented and are not empirically calibrated. Sources: Tournier et al. (2020), Knott et al. (2020).

Two-enzyme PETase–MHETase system

Evidence: PETase and MHETase have complementary roles in PET depolymerisation. Game mechanism: A mass-conserving, two-compartment PET → MHET → product model. Caveat: Real PETase generates a mixture of products, not solely MHET, and the game simplifies the reaction network. Source: Knott et al. (2020).

Temperature and enzyme activity

Evidence: Enzyme-catalysed reactions proceed within a limited temperature range. The species description for Ideonella sakaiensis gives growth between 15°C and 42°C with an optimum of 30–37°C, and engineered PET hydrolases have been designed to operate at higher temperatures. Game mechanism: Three temperature settings (10°C, 30°C, 45°C), chosen to sit below, inside and above the published growth range. At 30°C the reactor runs; at either extreme the modelled rate falls to roughly 2% and the tank visibly stalls. Caveat: The three-step choice and the size of the penalty are teaching simplifications, not measured activity data, and 45°C is not universally unfavourable — engineered enzymes such as FAST-PETase are designed to work warmer. Sources: Tanasupawat et al. (2016), Lu et al. (2022).

Nomenclature

The organism has since been reclassified as Pseudideonella sakaiensis. This game and its reference list keep the name Ideonella sakaiensis, because that is the name used throughout the cited literature, including the original species description.

Experimental design and uncertainty

Principle: Replication, controls and measurement variability are important when assessing evidence. Game mechanism: Synthetic control and enzyme-treatment series with means and sample standard deviation bars. Caveat: All plotted values are generated by a teaching model and are not taken from published experiments.

Hydraulic gates, magnetic collection, drones and fouling

Game abstraction: The interactive gates, combat drones, airborne shockwaves, and simplified machinery are fictional or idealised engineering tools. The magnetic collector works on hypothetically tagged carriers; it does not imply that unmodified PET is ferromagnetic. None of these action-game powers should be treated as a validated industrial process.

Tank temperature and the rate of fouling

Evidence: Biofilm-forming organisms in industrial water systems are largely mesophilic, so fouling accumulates fastest in the same moderate range that suits most cultured bacteria and is suppressed at low temperatures and by thermal treatment. Game mechanism: The speed of the moving fouling is multiplied by a curve peaking at 30°C and falling to roughly 0.54 at 10°C and 0.65 at 45°C, so the temperature dial is a trade-off rather than a single right answer: the setting that lets the enzymes work is also the setting the fouling likes. Caveat: The curve is invented and deliberately shallow, so that sitting at an extreme never beats running the reactor properly. Movement speed is a visual stand-in for growth rate; real fouling does not swim. Mode note: The five machines and two tactical stations are drawn and operable in Advanced mode only. In Explorer mode the drone’s power is floored and scanning is automatic, which made that equipment redundant; in Advanced mode power is scarce and the equipment is needed. In neither mode does the hull or the containment seal regenerate: both bars fall under damage and are restored only at the repair terminal, which is free in Explorer mode and costs 20 charge in Advanced.

Biosafety and environmental release

Design safeguard: No cultivated organisms are intentionally released into nature. Sample retrieval and material sorting happen outside the sealed reactor; cultured microbes are kept inside containment. The airlock is a safety abstraction. Neither the game nor its scenarios provide real-world cultivation or release instructions.

Age suitability, accessibility and evaluation

The interface is built for ages 8–12: one guided mode, two controls (move and clean), plain-English labels in place of technical terms, and large touch targets. The technical vocabulary is kept in small print beside the plain wording. It has not yet been formally tested with children, which remains the main outstanding evaluation step. Local pass-and-play co-op is a prototype and requires user testing for readability, pacing, accessibility and learning outcomes before a classroom deployment.

What is real, and what is invented?

Evidence-based: Ideonella sakaiensis and its PETase/MHETase enzyme system; PET as a particular polymer; multi-stage depolymerisation; substrate specificity; dependence on process conditions; engineering of PET hydrolases.

Game abstractions: C-7 drone, tractor pulse, giant biofilm encounters, material “units”, wave mechanics, flow-control powers, upgrades, and the numeric rates of processing or damage. The clock runs about one simulated day every two and a half seconds, a deliberate narrative time compression, not a claim about measured PET biodegradation rates An on-screen note inside the game states that the process is sped up and that real bacteria need weeks, so players are not left thinking PET breaks down in seconds.

Safety decision: No cultured bacteria are released into a river. Collected PET enters a contained bioreactor; non-PET is diverted to separate sorting. The visually realistic environment is a fictional AI-generated chamber illustration, not a scientific micrograph.

Educational objective

Players should be able to explain that bacteria are diverse microscopic organisms, that only certain enzymes act on certain materials, and that PET processing requires identification, controlled industrial conditions and realistic timescales. The action-game enemies symbolize unwanted fouling of equipment, not a particular dangerous bacterial species.

Programming tools, web technologies and creative credits

Languages and standards

HTML5 provides the document structure, CSS3 provides the responsive interface, and vanilla JavaScript (ECMAScript) implements the game logic, simulation, audio and menus. HTML Canvas 2D provides rendering of moving units and effects. Browser Pointer Events support mouse and touchscreen controls. Web Audio API generates the three original instrumental themes. Web Storage (localStorage) saves settings and game progress; Blob and File APIs support save export and import.

Libraries and engines

External runtime libraries: none. This build uses native browser APIs and does not include Phaser, Unity, Unreal Engine, React, a Spotify SDK, or a commercial music library. A compatible modern browser is required.

Development and testing

The current revision used Python 3 for source editing and packaging and Chromium with Playwright for automated browser interaction tests. This document lists development technology separately from the research citations that underpin biological claims. Browser tests do not replace testing with 8–12-year-old players or on physical Android hardware.

Visual and music asset disclosure

The microscopic chamber and sprite images are fictional, AI-assisted visual artworks, combined with original programmatic 2D game effects. They are not scientific micrographs. The optional Deep Current, Discovery Pulse and Containment Signal game soundtracks are original synthesised sequences produced using the Web Audio API; no third-party music recordings are redistributed.

Spotify external-player option

Players can paste their own playlist, album or track URL from Spotify. The game opens the link in a separate tab or app and does not request account access, play music inside the game, or synchronize Spotify tracks with gameplay. Spotify requires internet and its availability is controlled by Spotify. See Spotify developer documentation.

Explorer Mode: age-appropriate gameplay

The default guided mode offers three goals per stage and a two-action control scheme: move and clean. Material is collected automatically when reached; correct sorting happens automatically at the destination station; optional scientific systems remain accessible separately. The fully featured Advanced Mode is preserved for older players.

Final reward: completing the final biofilm encounter and PET-processing objectives displays a congratulatory victory screen with totals and three science lessons.
